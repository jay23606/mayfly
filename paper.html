Snaps Without
a Server

How mayfly moves disappearing media between people without making a service the permanent middleman.

mayfly vs. a store-and-forward modelCompanion: The Zero-Server StackSource code
Short version

Most messaging systems upload media to a service, wait for the recipient, and delete it later. mayfly prefers a direct WebRTC transfer: the full Snap travels from one browser to the other. When that is impossible, it uses a small, recipient-encrypted photo relay with strict limits. The gain is less readable media on the server; the cost is weaker offline delivery, network reliability, moderation, and multi-device recovery.

01The design decision

Where does the full image wait?

A conventional disappearing-media service is still a store-and-forward system. The full image is uploaded, retained until delivery or expiry, then removed under the provider’s rules. That model is dependable: the recipient can be asleep, offline, or on a difficult network. It also means the provider operates the media path.

mayfly keeps the frontend on GitHub Pages and uses Supabase for accounts, metadata, real-time signaling, encrypted relay storage, and row-level access control. The media path is different: when both people are online, the full Snap moves directly between their browsers over WebRTC. The database sees a small preview and the information needed to route the event, not the full live image.

02Two delivery routes

Recipient online

  • Mayfly stores the full photo or video in the sender’s browser.
  • Supabase Realtime carries the connection handshake and a small Snap row.
  • The recipient opens the Snap and pulls full media over WebRTC.
  • Opening records a receipt; timed Snaps are removed after their timer.

Recipient offline

  • Photo only: video, voice clips, and file attachments remain live-only.
  • The photo is re-encoded into a bounded WebP/JPEG relay copy, encrypted to the recipient’s public key, then stored as ciphertext.
  • When the recipient returns, their browser downloads and decrypts it locally.
  • The relay is removed when opened or when its delivery window expires.

The offline path is not a full cloud inbox. It is an intentionally constrained escape hatch for a photo that cannot be delivered live.

03Current bounds are part of the design

Mayfly limits the server-side fallback rather than pretending it is a replacement for a media backend.

FeatureCurrent behavior
Offline photo relayRecipient-encrypted, re-encoded to ≤50 KB; one unopened relay per recipient and 100 outstanding relays per sender; expires after seven days.
Live SnapsFull-quality peer-to-peer photo or video; normal Snap rows expire after 24 hours.
Stories24-hour posts; five newest per account; an offline preview is capped at 20 KB while the full story remains peer-to-peer.
Direct textEnd-to-end encrypted in transit and at rest; at most ten undelivered messages per offline recipient, with a seven-day TTL.
Group media and callsLive peer-to-peer mesh. Group text uses member-only Realtime broadcast and is not durable history.

05The honest trade

What mayfly gains

  • Live full media does not sit in an application media store.
  • Relay media is encrypted to the intended recipient.
  • Media bandwidth and storage needs stay small for a private, human-scale app.
  • The stack is compact: static hosting, managed database services, and browser APIs.

What mayfly gives up

  • No guaranteed full-quality offline photo or video delivery.
  • No TURN relay yet, so direct peer connections can fail on restrictive or cellular networks.
  • No server-side content moderation pipeline or rich cloud features.
  • Device-local keys and media make recovery and multi-device use harder.
Important boundary

Neither architecture can prevent a recipient from taking a screenshot or recording a screen. “Ephemeral” describes delivery and retention behavior, not control over what a human does after viewing.

06Conclusion

mayfly is not a replacement for a global social platform. It is a different answer to a narrower problem: private, disappearing sharing among people who are usually online. The core claim is modest: a service does not need to hold every full image in order to provide Snaps, Stories, chat, groups, and calls. For that product shape, direct delivery plus a bounded encrypted fallback is a useful architectural default.
